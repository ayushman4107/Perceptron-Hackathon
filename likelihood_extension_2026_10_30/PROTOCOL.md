# Frozen pilot protocol: likelihood-based policy comparison

This experiment tests a practical extension to the current OPE project, not a claim that likelihood-ratio inference is new. Written before running numerical comparisons. Date: 30 October 2026.

Question: can a shared Bernoulli likelihood region retain finite-sample, simultaneous policy-comparison validity while resolving more beneficial changes than cellwise confidence boxes? Reference policy is the actual logger in every valid-design scenario, correcting the previous candidate's scope limitation.

Primary: 8 equally weighted context strata, binary actions, logger P(A=1)=.1, q0=.002 and q1=.02 in each stratum; n=60,000, 32 prespecified candidates including the logger and always-action-1. 500 independent logs summarized by exact binomial/multinomial sufficient statistics. Additional stochastic policies are mixtures of logger and always-1 with context-specific coefficients generated before outcomes (seed 915). The primary metric is beneficial-change certification rate versus full-data cellwise KL-Chernoff intervals, not versus Hoeffding alone. Secondary metrics: selected true gain, false improvement, harmful selection, whole-family coverage, interval width, runtime, and width/decision differences against a full-data mixture-likelihood region.

Candidate: fit Jeffreys-smoothed cell means on half of the observations; use their likelihood on the other half as numerator in a split likelihood-ratio confidence set. Project that joint region onto each policy contrast. This is an application of universal inference, with a specialized separable convex solver. New algorithmic novelty is NOT established by this construction.

Baselines: (1) full-data cellwise KL-Chernoff box, (2) full-data Hoeffding box, (3) full-data Jeffreys-mixture likelihood region, (4) full-data tabular Wald with Bonferroni correction over candidate contrasts, (5) always retain logger. The mixture region is an essential strong alternative, not a deliberately weak comparator. No claim of reproducing CoinDICE or empirical-likelihood OPE; those have different models/objectives.

All five inferential methods use the same log budget, known context probabilities and fixed candidate family. Split methods pay for the pilot within the budget. Candidate selection maximizes lower contrast and requires it to exceed 1e-10. A shared confidence region covers all policy contrasts on its fixed cells; there is no additional policy-count factor. Cellwise intervals are simultaneous over all cells. Wald uses an explicit policy-count correction, but remains asymptotic and may fail in sparse cells.

Scenarios: rare homogeneous rewards (primary); regular homogeneous rewards (.2,.4); null rare rewards (.01,.01); heterogeneous gains (q0=.1, q1 alternates .25 and .02); weak overlap (q0=.1,q1=.2, logger .001); high-dimensional rare rewards (32 strata); no-overlap (logger always0); and hidden-confounding stress (latent equiprobable U; e(U)=.1/.9, q1(U)=.1/.9, q0=.6; observed A1 mean .82 but causal .5). Each uses n in {6000,60000}, 32 candidates, 500 repetitions: 8,000 independent logs. Primary stratum count is 8; missing action cells retain [0,1] ambiguity. Bernoulli outcomes, IID rows, fixed logger, sufficient strata and known target context weights are required for causal conclusions. No robustness to hidden confounding is claimed.

Primary success gate: paired beneficial-decision gain >=10 percentage points over full-data KL-box, paired 95% Monte Carlo interval lower endpoint >0, and no observed false-improvement excess inconsistent with alpha=.05 in valid null designs. This is an empirical gate, not a statistical proof of universal superiority. If the candidate fails or a simpler strong alternative wins, report that and prefer the justified alternative. Do not tune on the frozen seeds. Worst-case proportion MCSE is 2.24 percentage points for 500 repetitions. Report Wilson intervals including zero-event trials.

Seeds: 2200000 + 100000*scenario_index + 10000*sample_size_index + repetition. Same sufficient statistics and candidate family for every method within a trial. The simulator samples half-log multinomial cell counts then binomial successes independently for each half; this is exactly the distribution of sufficient statistics of the declared IID tabular log, not access to unobserved rewards. Oracle q only enters generator and metrics.

Tests before experiments: KL identities/domains; one-cell inversion; analytic dual versus independently enumerated two-cell grid; feasible primal/dual agreement; missing-support range; identical-policy zero; exact small-sample likelihood-ratio expectation; reproducibility; and true-region containment. Any numerical discrepancy blocks the benchmark. Ordinary floating-point tolerances are documented; no verified-arithmetic claim is made.

No public-data evaluation, continuous-context representation learning, estimated context-mass inference, general reward-model misspecification, sequential stopping, real acquisition or user-interface build is included. Published likelihood methods remain acknowledged prior work. The study can support a defensible application advantage; it cannot establish uniqueness against unknown competing teams.
